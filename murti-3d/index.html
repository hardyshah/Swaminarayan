About these models

These are stylised, hand-sculpted models made from darshan photographs, not 3D scans. A true copy of a murti needs dozens of photos from all sides in even light (or a laser scan), so faces, proportions and ornaments here are an approximation. The shringar shown is one representative set; in the mandir it changes every day.

Printing: supports are needed under the flute or raised hand, the crown and the garland. For 36 inch and 1 metre, cut the model into parts in your slicer and print hollow.

About the STEP files: they are converted from the sculpted mesh, so the surface is about 100,000 small flat facets, not smooth CAD surfaces, and there are no editable features. They open as one closed solid in Fusion, SolidWorks, FreeCAD, Onshape and similar programs, which is useful for adding a base or name plate, cutting into parts or measuring. They are coarser than the STL and can be slow, so use the STL for printing.
