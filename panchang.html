પંચાંગ અને તેનું વિજ્ઞાનThe Panchang, and the science behind it

A panchang (પંચ + અંગ, "five limbs") describes each day with five numbers: tithi, vaar, nakshatra, yoga and karana. Four of them come from just two things you can see in the sky: where the sun is, and where the moon is. The fifth, the vaar, comes from the seven moving lights the ancients could see with the naked eye. Below, today's five limbs run live, then the sky that produces them, then each limb explained.

પાંચ અંગ, એક પછી એકThe five limbs, one by one

તિથિTithi, the lunar day

tithi = ⌊ (moon − sun) ÷ 12° ⌋ + 1  → 1 … 30

When the moon has pulled 12° ahead of the sun, one tithi is over. Thirty tithis make one lunar month, from new moon to new moon: 15 in the bright half (sud, waxing) and 15 in the dark half (vad, waning).

Why a tithi is 19 to 26 hours long. The moon's orbit is an ellipse. Near its closest point (perigee) it moves up to 15° a day and a tithi is short; near its farthest point it slows to about 12° a day and a tithi is long. The sun's speed varies a little too.

Skipped and repeated tithis. The day takes the name of the tithi running at sunrise. A short tithi that starts after one sunrise and ends before the next is never "at sunrise", so it is skipped (kshaya). A long tithi that covers two sunrises names two days (vriddhi). That is why the panchang sometimes jumps from 3 to 5, or has two 11s.

Why it is clever. A tithi is a clock you can read by looking up: the moon's phase tells you the tithi to within a day, anywhere in the world, with no instrument.

વારVaar, the weekday

day's lord = lord of the first hora after sunrise

The seven naked-eye "planets" (grahas) are placed in order of how slowly they move against the stars: Shani, Guru, Mangal, Surya, Shukra, Budh, Chandra. Each hour (hora) of the day is ruled by the next one in this ring.

A day has 24 horas. 24 = 3 × 7 + 3, so the first hora of the next day lands three steps further round the ring. Follow the star line, jumping three each time, and you get Ravi → Som → Mangal → Budh → Guru → Shukra → Shani: the week, in the same order used all over the world.

The vaar runs from sunrise to sunrise, not midnight to midnight.

નક્ષત્રNakshatra, the moon's star

nakshatra = ⌊ moon (sidereal) ÷ 13°20′ ⌋ + 1  → 1 … 27

The moon takes 27.32 days to go once round the stars (the sidereal month), so the sky was split into 27 equal parts of 13°20′, one for each night. Each is named after a bright star or group in it: Krittika is the Pleiades, Rohini is Aldebaran, Chitra is Spica.

Because the moon's speed varies, a nakshatra lasts about 20 to 27 hours. Each nakshatra has 4 padas of 3°20′, and 27 × 4 = 108, which fits the 12 rashis exactly (9 padas each).

યોગYoga, sun plus moon

yoga = ⌊ (sun + moon) ÷ 13°20′ ⌋ + 1  → 1 … 27

Where the tithi uses the difference between moon and sun, the yoga uses their sum. Both are measured against the stars. The sum grows by about 14° a day, so a yoga lasts roughly a day and there are 27 of them.

Two yogas, Vyatipata and Vaidhriti, mark moments when the sun and moon are at equal declination on opposite sides of a solstice or equinox, which is why the old texts single them out.

કરણKarana, half a tithi

karana = ⌊ (moon − sun) ÷ 6° ⌋ + 1  → 1 … 60

Each tithi is split into two karanas, so a month has 60. There are only 11 names: four fixed ones that fall once a month around the new moon (Shakuni, Chatushpada, Naga, Kimstughna), and seven moving ones that repeat eight times through the other 56 halves.

Vishti, also called Bhadra, is the karana panchangs warn about when choosing a time to start something.

માસ અને અધિક માસMonths and the adhik maas

12 × 29.531 = 354.37 days  vs  365.26 days → 10.9 days short each year

A Gujarati month runs from new moon to new moon (amanta). Each lunar month is named after the sankranti, the sun's entry into a new rashi, that happens during it. Chaitra is the month in which the sun enters Mesh.

Twelve lunar months fall about 11 days short of a solar year. After about 32½ months the gap adds up to a whole month, and one lunar month passes with no sankranti at all. That month has no name of its own, so it is called adhik ("extra") and the next month takes the name. This keeps Diwali in autumn and Holi in spring for ever. There are 7 adhik months every 19 years.

બે નવાં વર્ષWhy there are two Hindu new years

The same Vikram Samvat is counted from two starting points. Most of North India begins the year on Chaitra sud 1, the first new moon after the sun nears the spring equinox (Hindu Nav Varsh, Gudi Padwa, Ugadi). Gujarat begins it on Kartak sud 1, the day after Diwali (Bestu varas). Both mark a new moon close to a turning point of the sun's year.

નિરયન અને સાયનFixed stars, and the slowly drifting Sankranti

The panchang measures the sun against the fixed stars (nirayan, sidereal). Western calendars measure it from the spring equinox (sayan, tropical). The Earth's axis wobbles like a slowing top, one turn every ~25,800 years, so the equinox slides back against the stars by 50.3″ a year. The gap between the two zodiacs is the ayanamsa.
