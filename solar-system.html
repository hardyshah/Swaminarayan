Sun at centre

How the planets really move: all the same way round, the inner ones faster. The ring outside is the zodiac, fixed to the stars. Tick "Hold the Earth–Sun line still" and the stars drift round once a year instead: that is why different rashis are up at night in different seasons.
ભૂકેન્દ્રી

Earth at centre

The sky as we see it, and as jyotish reckons it. The sun goes round the rashis once a year, the moon once in 27.3 days. Each planet draws loops: where a loop turns back, the planet is vakri (retrograde). Run it at 1 month/s and watch Mars.
આકાશગંગામાં

Through the galaxy

The sun is not still. It carries the planets round the centre of the Milky Way at about 200 km every second, so each planet's path is a long spring. The arrow shows the way we are going; the earth's axis keeps pointing at Dhruva all the while.

ધ્રુવ અને આકાશગંગાThe pole star and the Milky Way

Dhruva and the 25,800-year wobble

The earth spins like a top, and its axis points at one spot in the sky. Today that spot is beside Polaris, our Dhruva tara, so it stays still while every other star circles it each night. But the top wobbles: the axis draws a circle among the stars once in about 25,800 years. Move the slider to see which star was, or will be, the pole star.

This same wobble moves the equinox back through the rashis by 1° in about 72 years. That slow slide is the ayanamsha, now about 24°, the gap between the Indian (nirayana) and Western (sayana) zodiacs.

Our place in the Milky Way

  • The sun is about 26,000 light-years from the centre, which lies in Dhanu (Sagittarius), near Mula nakshatra.
  • It moves at about 200 km/s (720,000 km/h) and takes about 230 million years for one lap.
  • The planets' plane is tilted about 60° to the galaxy's plane, so we travel nearly "pole first", not flat like a plate.
  • Polaris is 430 light-years away and goes round the galaxy with us. That is why Dhruva stays in place for thousands of years even at this speed; only the earth's own wobble changes the pole star.

The Vishnu Purana and the Bhagavat describe the stars turning about Dhruva like oxen about a threshing post. That is a true picture of the nightly sky. The motion round the galaxy was found only in the 20th century and is not in the old texts.
