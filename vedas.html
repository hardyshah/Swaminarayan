ચાર વેદThe four Vedas

The Vedas are the oldest scriptures of the Hindu tradition. In the Vachanamrut, Shriji Maharaj names the Vedas again and again as the root authority, alongside the Shrimad Bhagavat and the other shastras: the word વેદ appears 43 times in the Vachanamrut, and શ્રુતિ 33 times.

જોડાણHow the four Vedas share verses

Many Rigveda verses are repeated in the other three Vedas, word for word or with small changes. The Samaveda takes almost all its verses from the Rigveda and sets them to music. VedAnvaya compared every verse with every other verse and recorded each match as one of a few stated kinds. In the reader, a Rigveda verse with a match shows a ↔ chip.

Same text
Identical once spelling conventions are set aside.
Reuses the text
The Samaveda verse is taken from this Rigveda verse.
Variant
The same verse with a few words changed.
Near parallel
Most of the wording is shared. Shared vocabulary alone is not counted on this page.

Counts are VedAnvaya's, for one recension of each Veda: Rigveda Shakala, Samaveda Kauthuma (archika), Yajurveda Vajasaneyi Madhyandina, Atharvaveda Shaunaka.

વેદ અને આધુનિક વિજ્ઞાનThe Vedas, modern science and modern life

People make many claims about the Vedas and science. Some are solid, some are fair readings, and some are not supported by the texts or by evidence. Each card below says which, links the verse so you can read it yourself, and cites its sources.

✓ Established≈ Fair reading? Speculative✗ Not supported♡ Value for life

Established = in the text and agreed by scholars, or measured in published studies. Fair reading = the text supports it but other readings exist. Speculative = an analogy or a modern reading the text does not clearly make. Not supported = checked and found wrong. Value for life = guidance, not a scientific claim.

ગણિતMathematics and computing

✓ Established

Geometry for building fire altars

The Shulba Sutras, manuals attached to the Yajurveda for laying out altars, state the rule we now call Pythagoras' theorem, list whole-number triangles such as 3-4-5 and 5-12-13, and give √2 as 1 + 1/3 + 1/(3·4) − 1/(3·4·34) = 1.4142156…, correct to five decimals. Baudhayana's sutra is dated about 800–500 BCE.

Today: the same rule is used by every builder squaring a corner with a 3-4-5 rope.

📖 Shulba Sutras📖 MacTutor: The Indian Sulbasutras
✓ Established

Binary numbers from Vedic metres

Pingala's Chandahshastra, the Vedanga on metre, lists every pattern of short (laghu) and long (guru) syllables. Its method is the binary number system, and its "Meru prastara" is the triangle later called Pascal's. Counting the metres also gives the Fibonacci numbers, worked out by Virahanka and Hemachandra centuries before Fibonacci.

Today: every computer counts in the same two symbols.

📖 Pingala📖 Fibonacci sequence: history
✓ Established

Grammar rules like a programming language

Panini's grammar (about 4th century BCE), written to protect the Vedic language, packs Sanskrit into about 4,000 short rules with meta-rules, much like the notation used to define programming languages. In 1967 the computer scientist P. Z. Ingerman proposed calling that notation "Panini–Backus form".

📖 Ingerman 1967, Communications of the ACM📖 Panini
✓ Established

Names for very large numbers

The Yajurveda (Vajasaneyi Samhita 17.2) names powers of ten, one after another, up to parardha = 1012. Few ancient cultures named numbers that large. The later place-value system with zero grew from this habit of counting in tens.

📖 White Yajurveda book 17, Griffith📖 Indian numbering system
✗ Not supported

"Vedic Mathematics" is in the Vedas

The book Vedic Mathematics (1965) by Bharati Krishna Tirtha teaches useful mental arithmetic tricks, but its 16 sutras are not found in any Veda. The author said he reconstructed them; scholars have found no Vedic source. The tricks work; the label is wrong.

📖 Vedic Mathematics (book)

ખગોળ અને સમયAstronomy and time

✓ Established

A year of 12 months and 360 days

"The fellies are twelve; the wheel is one … within it are collected 360 spokes." RV 1.164.48

"The twelve-spoked wheel … seven hundred and twenty children in pairs abide in it." RV 1.164.11

The wheel is the year: 12 months of 30 days, and 720 = 360 days + 360 nights. A 360-day ritual year with extra days added to keep up with the sun is the start of the luni-solar calendar still used in every panchang.

Panchang and its scienceVedic time📖 Vedanga Jyotisha
≈ Fair reading

The moon shines with the sun's light

"The solar rays found the light of Tvashta concealed in the mansion of the moving moon." RV 1.84.15

Yaska's Nirukta (about 5th century BCE) explains such verses as saying one ray of the sun lights the moon. That matches what we know. The verse itself is poetic, so other readings exist.

📖 Nirukta📖 NASA: Moon phases
✓ Established

Ancient astronomy that still matches measurement

Later Indian astronomy, built on the Vedanga Jyotisha, measured the sidereal year to within minutes and the lunar month to within a second. The Panchang page sets those numbers against NASA's.

📖 Surya Siddhanta📖 NASA Moon fact sheet
? Speculative

Dating the Vedas from star positions

B. G. Tilak (The Orion, 1893) argued from references to the spring equinox in Orion that some hymns are over 6,000 years old. The verses can be read other ways, and most scholars date the Rigveda to about 1500–1200 BCE from language and archaeology. The question is open, but the early date is not established.

📖 Rigveda: dating📖 Tilak, The Orion (archive.org)

ભૌતિક વિજ્ઞાન અને બ્રહ્માંડPhysics and the universe

✓ Established

The hymn that asks how the universe began

"The non-existent was not, the existent was not … Who really knows? … whence was this creation?" RV 10.129.1 · 10.129.6

The Nasadiya Sukta asks what came before space and time, and ends by saying even the highest may not know. That is the question cosmologists still ask about the time before the Big Bang. The hymn asks the question; it does not give a physical theory.

📖 Nasadiya Sukta📖 NASA: The Big Bang
✓ Established

Physicists who read the Vedic texts

Erwin Schrödinger, a founder of quantum mechanics, wrote about the Upanishads' idea of one consciousness in What is Life? (1944) and My View of the World (1961). Carl Sagan said in Cosmos (1980) that the Hindu time scales, a day of Brahma of 8.64 billion years, are the only ones of an ancient faith that match modern cosmology. This is history: they were inspired by the ideas, not proving them.

📖 Erwin Schrödinger📖 Cosmos: A Personal Voyage (episode 10)Vedic time scales
≈ Fair reading

Rta: one order behind all nature

"Earth is upheld by truth; heaven is upheld by the sun." RV 10.85.1

Rta (ऋत) is the order that keeps the sun, seasons and rivers on course, and the same word means moral truth. The idea that one lawful order runs through all of nature is the starting belief of physics. It is a parallel in outlook, not a physical law.

📖 Rta
? Speculative

The "golden egg" and the Big Bang

"Hiranyagarbha was present at the beginning …" RV 10.121.1

Some compare the golden embryo from which everything arose to the hot, dense early universe. It is a striking image, but the hymn is about a creator god, and the likeness is a modern analogy, not something the text says.

📖 Hiranyagarbha
? Speculative

The speed of light in a Rigveda commentary

"You, Surya, outstrip all in speed … you are the source of light." RV 1.50.4

Sayana's 14th-century commentary on this verse says the sun travels 2,202 yojanas in half a nimesha. With one choice of units that comes close to the speed of light. But the length of a yojana and of a nimesha varied, the line is in the commentary and not the hymn, and it may describe the sun's motion, so it proves nothing.

📖 S. Kak, The speed of light and Puranic cosmology (arXiv)
✗ Not supported

Aeroplanes and nuclear weapons in the Vedas

The Vaimanika Shastra, often cited for Vedic aircraft, was written down in the early 20th century. A 1974 study at the Indian Institute of Science found its designs could not fly. No Veda describes aircraft or atomic weapons.

📖 Vaimanika Shastra

મન, શ્વાસ અને આરોગ્યMind, breath and health

✓ Established

Chanting slows the breath and calms the heart

A study in the British Medical Journal (2001) found that reciting a yoga mantra, like reciting the rosary, slowed breathing to about six breaths a minute and strengthened healthy heart-rate rhythms. The study was small (23 adults), but the effect was clear.

📖 Bernardi et al. 2001, BMJ
≈ Early evidence

Om chanting and the brain

A pilot brain scan study (2011, 12 people) saw calmer activity in emotion areas of the brain while chanting "Om", compared with saying "ssss". It is a small first study, not proof.

📖 Kalyani et al. 2011, Int J Yoga
≈ Early evidence

Memorising the Vedas and memory in the brain

Scans of 21 professional Vedic pandits found more grey matter in memory regions than in matched controls. The study shows a link, not that chanting caused it, and the group was small.

📖 Hartzell et al. 2016, NeuroImage📖 Scientific American: the "Sanskrit effect"
✓ Established

The Atharvaveda and the roots of Ayurveda

The Atharvaveda has hymns to healing plants and against fever, cough and skin disease, and Ayurveda counts itself an upaveda of it. Some plants have real medical value; others have not held up. Turmeric's curcumin, for example, has been tested hard and is a poor drug on its own.

📖 Atharvaveda📖 Nelson et al. 2017, J Med Chem (curcumin)
? Speculative

Yajna smoke cleans the air

One 2007 study burned a mix of herbs with wood and found fewer airborne bacteria in a closed room for a day. It was one study, not repeated widely, and any smoke also adds fine particles that harm the lungs. Claims that havan or Agnihotra cure pollution are not supported.

📖 Nautiyal et al. 2007, J Ethnopharmacology📖 WHO: smoke and health
? Speculative

What plant was Soma?

Soma, the pressed drink of over a thousand verses (see the Soma juice theme), has been identified as ephedra, a mushroom, and many other plants. No identification is agreed.

📖 Soma (drink)

જ્ઞાન-સંરક્ષણ અને સંગીતInformation, memory and music

✓ Established

3,000 years of copying without a printer

The Vedas were passed on by mouth. Students learned each verse in several patterns: word by word (pada), in pairs (krama), and back and forth (jata, ghana). Any slip shows up as a broken pattern, the same idea as a checksum in computing. UNESCO lists Vedic chanting as an intangible heritage of humanity.

📖 UNESCO: Tradition of Vedic chanting📖 Vedic chant
✓ Established

From Samaveda chant to the seven notes

The Samaveda's sung verses use pitch marks, and Indian music theory (the Natya Shastra and later works) traces the seven svaras (sa re ga ma pa dha ni) back to this chanting.

📖 Samaveda📖 Svara

આજના જીવનમાંFor life today

♡ Value for life

Teamwork: meet, speak and think together

"Meet together, talk together, let your minds apprehend alike." RV 10.191.2 · 10.191.4

The last hymn of the Rigveda is about agreement. It is still chanted at the end of meetings and is a good rule for any team, family or mandal.

♡ Value for life

An open mind

"May auspicious works come to us from every quarter." RV 1.89.1

"Learned priests call one by many names." RV 1.164.46

Welcoming good ideas from anywhere is also how science grows.

♡ Value for life

Gambling and addiction

"Play not with dice; pursue agriculture; delight in wealth so acquired." RV 10.34.13

The Gambler's Hymn (RV 10.34) describes a man who has lost his family and self-respect to dice and cannot stop. It is one of the oldest descriptions of addiction, and its advice is honest work.

♡ Value for life

Giving does not make you poorer

"The riches of one who gives do not diminish." RV 10.117.1

Hymn 10.117 praises feeding the hungry. Studies of giving find that spending on others raises happiness.

📖 Dunn et al. 2008, Science
♡ Value for life

Earth is our mother

"The earth is my mother, I am her son." Atharvaveda 12.1.12

The Prithvi Sukta (AV 12.1) asks that what we dig from the earth grow back quickly. It reads like an early call for care of the environment.

📖 AV 12.1, Griffith
♡ Value for life

Prayer for a clear mind

"We meditate on that desirable light of the divine Savita, who influences our pious rites." RV 3.62.10

The Gayatri mantra asks for a bright intellect, not for wealth. Start the day with it, then study.

ધ્યાન રાખોClaims to be careful with

  • ✗ "The Vedas contain modern physics equations." No verse states a physical law or an equation. Parallels on this page are in ideas and questions.
  • ✗ "The seven horses of the sun are the seven colours of light." The Rigveda's sun has seven horses, but nothing in the text links them to a spectrum; that link is modern.
  • ? "Exact dates for Vedic events from astronomy." Possible in principle, but every published date depends on a chosen reading of poetic verses.
  • ✓ What is solid: careful arithmetic and geometry for the altar, a calendar of 12 months and 360 days, a system of oral copying that kept the text intact for 3,000 years, and deep questions about where everything came from.
