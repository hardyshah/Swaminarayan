Vedic time starts at sunrise and divides the day by the sun, so some units stretch in summer and shrink in winter. Clock time starts at midnight, and every hour is the same length all year. શતપથ બ્રાહ્મણનું માપThe older ladder, from the Shatapatha Brahmana

Long before the Bhagavat, the Shatapatha Brahmana (a later-Vedic text) built its own ladder up from the truti, with a muhurta of 48 minutes at its centre. Its units have different sizes from the Bhagavat's: its nimesha is about 57 thousandths of a second, while the Bhagavat's is about 0.43 s. Different texts define the small units differently, so the table names its source.

Notice the pattern: 15 × 15 × 15 × 15 steps, then 30, 30, 12. The 60-year yuga-sangha is the same length as the 60-year cycle of named years (samvatsara chakra) still printed in panchangs.

યુગ, કલ્પ અને બ્રહ્માનો દિવસYugas, kalpas and the day of Brahma

The same counting goes upward. A year of the devas is 360 human years. Four yugas, in the ratio 4 : 3 : 2 : 1, make a mahayuga of 43,20,000 years. A thousand mahayugas make one day of Brahma (a kalpa), 4.32 billion years, and his night is as long again.

One mahayuga = 43,20,000 years = 12,000 years of the devas. Each yuga includes a dawn and dusk (sandhya) of one tenth of its length.

Where we are now, by this reckoning

For comparison, science measures the Earth at about 4.54 billion years old and the universe at about 13.8 billion. A day of Brahma (4.32 billion years) is close to the Earth's age. That closeness is often pointed out, but the texts arrived at their numbers by counting cycles, not by measuring rocks, so it is best read as a striking coincidence rather than a prediction.

The units on this page were not written down all at once. Here is when each layer appears, using the dates scholars give (the tradition holds many of these texts to be far older).

▶ Preparation unplugged: Early and Later Vedic age, timeline📖 Vedic period
  1. c. 1500–1000 BCEEarly Vedic age: the RigvedaPictures the year as a wheel with 12 spokes and 360 parts (Rigveda 1.164.48), and counts months by the moon.
  2. c. 1000–500 BCELater Vedic age: Yajurveda and the BrahmanasThe 27 nakshatras are listed (Taittiriya Samhita 4.4.10). The Shatapatha Brahmana builds its truti-to-yuga ladder.
  3. dated variously, 1400–400 BCEVedanga Jyotisha of LagadhaThe first Indian text on astronomy for the calendar: a five-year yuga with added months to keep moon and sun in step.
  4. 57 BCE · 78 CEVikram Samvat and Shaka eras beginThe year counts still used in Gujarati panchangs today.
  5. c. 400–500 CESurya SiddhantaRevolution counts per mahayuga for the sun, moon and planets; the base of most panchang calculation for 1,500 years. See how accurate →
  6. 499 CEAryabhatiya of AryabhataSays the Earth turns on its axis; gives the sidereal day to within a hundredth of a second.
  7. c. 800–1000 CEShrimad Bhagavat Purana (scholars' dating)Skandh 3, adhyay 11 sets out the paramanu-to-Brahma ladder used in the tables above.
  8. 1150 CESiddhanta Shiromani of BhaskaracharyaRefines the Siddhanta methods; still taught in traditional schools.
  9. Chaitra sud 9, VS 1837 (1781 CE)Shri Swaminarayan bhagwan is born at ChhapaiyaHis life and the Vachanamrut are dated in exactly these units: Samvat, month, paksha and tithi.
